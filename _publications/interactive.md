abstract: >-
  DIY garment making has seen a resurgence in interest over the past three decades and offers potential benefits for sustainable clothing practices. However, prior research indicates that home sewers frequently experience garment fit challenges, leading to frustration and material waste. As digital tools become more widely adopted, there is a growing need to explore how such technologies can support improved fit outcomes and sewing workflows. This study conducted a needs assessment with 50 female home sewers to identify key challenges and inform design implications. Based on these findings, we developed an interactive size chart integrated with a fit-adjustment tool and evaluated it through an in-person pilot study using usability testing, user experience questionnaires, and interviews. Results suggest that well-designed digital tools embedded within the sewing workflow can significantly improve garment fit outcomes while enhancing efficiency and user satisfaction. Future work will focus on developing a standalone system that supports a wider range of garments and on conducting evaluations with a larger, more diverse participant sample.
